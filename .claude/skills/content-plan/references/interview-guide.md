# Interview guide

Question bank for the content-plan interview. Go topic by topic, 2–4 questions at a time, in Mariana's language. Skip anything already answered. Each topic notes *why it matters* so you can improvise good follow-ups instead of reading a script.

## 1. Goal and timeline

Why: the plan's phasing depends on urgency. Someone applying next week needs a different Phase 1 than someone exploring for six months.

- When does she want to start applying (or is she already)?
- What roles is she targeting? (AI Experience Designer, AI Content Strategist, Product Designer on AI teams…) Any dream companies or job posts she can share?
- Remote/international or local market? This changes language and salary framing.

## 2. Audience

Why: a portfolio for recruiters reads differently than one for design leads or freelance clients.

- Who does she most want to impress: recruiters, hiring design leads, founders, freelance clients?
- Is there a secondary audience (art community, e-commerce peers)?

## 3. Her story (feeds the About page)

Why: career changers win by making the transition feel inevitable, not desperate.

- The short version of her path: design → art → e-commerce content → AI. What's the thread that connects them?
- What does she want to be known for in two years?
- What makes her different from other designers claiming "AI skills"? (Push for specifics: real shipped work, scale, tools, taste.)

## 4. Work inventory (feeds case studies)

Why: this is the heart of the portfolio. Collect enough per project to judge which 2–4 make the strongest case studies.

For each project she mentions, capture:

- What was it, and for whom? Can the client be named, or does it need anonymizing?
- Her exact role — what did *she* decide, make, lead?
- Where did AI enter the workflow? Which tools, and what did she do that the tool didn't?
- Outcome: numbers if possible (cost, time, conversion, volume). If she doesn't know, note the metric worth chasing.
- What assets exist: images, before/afters, prototypes, live links, testimonials?

Then: which project is she proudest of, and which best matches the jobs she wants? (These may differ — the plan should say so.)

## 5. Art practice

Why: art differentiates her and shows taste, but mixed carelessly into client work it can blur the hiring story.

- Does she want art on the site? As its own gallery, woven into the journal, or only on social?
- Is any of it AI-assisted? That intersection (artist using generative tools with intent) is directly relevant to AI-experience roles.

## 6. Journal vs. blog

Why: the site has two writing collections. Without distinct purposes they will both die.

- Journal — short field notes: experiments, prompts, things she noticed working with AI. Low effort, high frequency. What would she actually enjoy logging?
- Blog — longer pieces: case-study write-ups, opinions, how-tos. What 2–3 topics could she write with authority *this month*?
- Honest cadence check: what rhythm can she sustain while job hunting?

## 7. Language

Why: it changes every piece of content and the market she reaches.

- Site in English, Spanish, or both? (If targeting US/EU salaries, English-first with Spanish where it adds reach is the common answer — but it's her call.)

## 8. Tone and references

Why: borrowed taste speeds up every later decision.

- 2–3 portfolio or personal sites she admires, and what specifically she likes about each.
- Words she'd want a stranger to use describing her site; words she'd hate.

## 9. Practical inventory

Why: small blockers stall launches.

- Final domain? Social profiles to link (LinkedIn, Instagram, Behance…)? Preferred contact method?
- Does her CV/resume match the positioning the plan proposes? If not, note it as a follow-up.
- Any deadlines (visa, applications, events) the roadmap must respect?
