# Designing the Ferrero digital shelf

**One designer · a portfolio of brands · many marketplaces · every content type — all on strict
global guidelines, in parallel, on deadline, and built to convert at thumbnail scale.**

> **A note on numbers:** the retailers and the brand own the sales analytics, so this case study
> isn't about a conversion stat I can claim. It's about the *decisions* — what changes per brand,
> per platform, and per moment, and why. For a digital shelf, that judgment is the job.

---

## The challenge

Ferrero runs a whole **portfolio of brands** on the digital shelf — Nutella, Kinder, Tic Tac,
Ferrero Rocher, Royal Dansk, Keebler, and more — each arriving with a thick global brand book and
zero tolerance for going off-guideline. That same content then has to live across **many
marketplaces — Amazon, Walmart, Target, Kroger, Instacart and others** — which each have different
image rules, layouts, and shopper behavior. And it has to work twice: once at full size on the
product page, and once as a **postage-stamp thumbnail** in a crowded search result, where most
purchase decisions actually start.

On top of that, the work runs **in parallel**: multiple brands and portfolios moving at the same
time, each with its own deadline — and none of them allowed to slip in quality or ship late.

My job: turn rigid brand guidelines into marketplace-specific shopping experiences that stay
unmistakably on-brand, earn the click and the add-to-cart, and land on time across the whole queue.

## My role

Digital Shelf Designer for Ferrero's portfolio. I design and optimize the full product-page stack —
hero images, basic content carousels, and enhanced (A+) content — plus seasonal campaigns and
Amazon Brand Stores, across many brands and many marketplaces, several projects at a time.

## 1. The everyday PDP — Nutella, end to end

The clearest way to show the work is one product, top to bottom. Here's the Nutella 13oz page as a
designed *sequence*, where every frame answers the next question a shopper has.

### Hero — survives the thumbnail, and adapts per marketplace
`[IMAGE: Nutella Everyday/Hero Images/00009800802203_B1N1.jpg]` *(Amazon)*
`[IMAGE: Nutella Everyday/Hero Images/00009800802203_B1N1_walmart.jpg]` *(Walmart)*

- The jar is sized and cropped so the **nutella wordmark stays legible when the image shrinks to a
  search-result thumbnail** — the real test, since that's where the decision starts.
- The **"7.7 oz" tag** and serving cues (toast, waffle, hazelnuts) communicate size and use *instantly*,
  before anyone reads a word.
- **The same hero gets a tailored version per marketplace** — each of Amazon, Walmart, Target,
  Kroger, Instacart has its own image rules and badge conventions. The two shown here (Amazon and
  Walmart) are one example of that adaptation; the difference between them *is* the point.

### Basic content carousel — a 5-step argument
`[IMAGE: Basic Content BC_001 → BC_005]`

The carousel isn't five nice pictures; it's an ordered argument:

1. **Appetite** — a rich, creamy texture close-up. Make them want it first.
2. **Emotion** — "Spread a Nutella Smile," a warm breakfast-table moment.
3. **Occasion** — "Perfect for Baking" expands *when* you'd use it (more uses = more value).
4. **Objection-handling** — "Only 7 Ingredients / No Artificial Colors / No Preservatives / Gluten
   Free." This is the trust frame, placed exactly where doubt creeps in.
5. **Choice** — "One Spread. Many Sizes." routes the shopper to the right pack instead of bouncing.

### Enhanced (A+) content — build the world, then cross-sell
`[IMAGE: Enhanced Content Mod1, Mod2a, Mod3 (recipe), Mod4 (range)]`

- The enhanced modules do what the basic carousel can't: **brand storytelling** ("Spread a Smile,"
  "Makes the Perfect Spread") for dwell time, a **full recipe module** (Blueberry Buttermilk Pancakes)
  that turns the page into content, and a **portfolio cross-sell** ("One Spread, Different Ways" —
  Nutella & Go, B-ready, biscuits).
- **The judgment:** basic content converts *this jar*; enhanced content builds the brand and grows
  basket size. I design them as two different jobs, not one long scroll.

## 2. Designing for the big moment — Kinder Bueno × Super Bowl

The everyday system is the baseline. The harder skill is dressing a brand for a cultural moment
**without losing the brand.**

`[IMAGE: Kinder Bueno Super Bowl/Hero/00009800000555_B1N1_SB.jpg]`
`[IMAGE: Kinder Bueno Super Bowl/Basic content kb/...BC_SEC_001.1.jpg]`
`[IMAGE: Kinder Bueno Super Bowl/Enhanced content kb/...EC_Mod1.jpg]`

- A **seasonal "skin"** — football-field blue, goalposts, footballs, "The Perfect Game Day Treat" —
  reads as *Super Bowl* in a half-second, even at thumbnail size.
- The **"$25K Each Week" Game Day sweepstakes** flash on the hero is a conversion lever tied to the
  moment, integrated into the pack graphic instead of slapped on top.
- Underneath the costume, **Kinder Bueno's wordmark, colors, and product truth stay sacred.** The
  three variants (regular, white, dark) are merchandised together to trade up the basket.
- **The judgment:** a seasonal layer should be loud enough to signal the occasion and quiet enough
  to keep the brand instantly recognizable. That tension is the whole design problem.

## 3. Range — same shelf, many different voices

Digital-shelf design isn't one look applied everywhere; it's knowing which **brand register** each
product lives in — across a portfolio that spans Nutella, Kinder, Tic Tac, Ferrero Rocher, Royal
Dansk, Keebler and more. A quick spread of the range:

- **World Cup — Ferrero Amazon Brand Store** `[IMAGE: Thumbnails/WORLDCUP - Ferrero Amazon Brandstore.jpg]`
  A full multi-section **storefront**, not a single PDP: "Enjoy the Match with Ferrero Favorites,"
  best-sellers, stock-up packs, "Discover all our iconic brands." This is navigation + merchandising
  for the whole portfolio.
- **Ferrero Rocher — Easter** `[IMAGE: Thumbnails/1. Ferrero Rocher_easter.jpg]`
  The **premium / gifting register** — gold, restraint, "Raise a Rocher," the hollow egg. Luxury voice.
- **Kinder Joy — Easter** `[IMAGE: Thumbnails/1. Kinder Joy_Easter.jpg]`
  The opposite end — **playful, kids-and-family** — "Treat + Toy," the app, "Create Memories That
  Last a Lifetime."

One designer moving fluently from luxury gold to kids' primary colors to game-day blue — on
guideline every time — is the takeaway.

## What I'd want you to notice

- I design the **whole funnel** (hero → carousel → enhanced → storefront), not isolated assets.
- I design for **the thumbnail first**, because that's where shoppers actually decide.
- I **translate constraints** — global brand books, each marketplace's rules, seasonal moments — into
  experiences that stay on-brand and still sell.
- I match the **register to the brand**: premium, playful, or everyday, deliberately.
- I do it **at volume and on deadline** — many brands across many marketplaces, several projects in
  flight at once, without letting quality slip.
