---
title: "Designing for trust at the payment step"
description: "A field note from the conversational checkout project: trust is built in the worst moments, not the best ones."
pubDate: 2026-06-12
tags: ["field-notes", "ai", "conversational-ux", "experience-design"]

Designing a conversational checkout assistant, I kept coming back to one idea: trust isn't built when
everything works. It's built when something breaks.

Anyone can write the happy-path copy. The design work is in the failure: the payment that didn't go
through, the expired coupon, the moment a user is one bad message away from leaving. That's where I
spent most of the effort — explain what happened in plain words, preserve the progress they already
made, offer a real alternative, and when the assistant can't fix it, hand off to a human *with the
conversation already transferred* so nobody has to repeat themselves.

The same is true for AI features generally. People decide whether to trust a system by how it behaves
when it's wrong. Design the recovery, not just the success.
