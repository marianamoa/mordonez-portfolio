title: "Designing for the thumbnail"
description: "A field note on the digital shelf: the real product page is the one that's two centimeters wide in a search result."
pubDate: 2026-06-10

Designing the Ferrero digital shelf taught me something that keeps showing up everywhere else: the
page most people actually "see" is the thumbnail in a crowded search result, not the full product
page you spent the day on.

So I started designing for that first. Can you still read the brand at two centimeters wide? Does the
hero say *what this is* and *how big it is* before anyone reads a word? If the answer's no, the
beautiful full-size page never gets the click that would show it.

It's a useful constraint far beyond e-commerce. Most interfaces have a "thumbnail" — the notification,
the list row, the half-second glance. Designing for the glance first, and the full view second, tends
to make both better.
