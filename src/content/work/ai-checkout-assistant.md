**A self-initiated project that does two things at once: it designs an *AI experience* — a conversational checkout assistant built around trust — and it was made with an *AI-assisted design process*, from research to high-fidelity screens.**

This is the kind of work my target roles ask for: designing systems where people need to understand, trust, and act on what software (and AI) tells them. I built it during the IBM *Generative AI for UI/UX* specialization, and used AI at every stage of the process — which is the point.

## The problem

Subscription checkout is where good products lose people: confusing pricing, long forms, payment anxiety, and overwhelming onboarding. I anchored the work on two personas:

- **Sofia — the Productivity Optimizer.** Needs speed and reliability; loses trust the moment a workflow breaks.
- **Marcus — the Socially Motivated Explorer.** Values affordability and simplicity; easily put off by aggressive paywalls.

The journey map surfaced the highest-friction moments — *Checkout* (too many steps), *Payment* (billing confusion, hesitation), and *Confirmation* (overwhelming onboarding) — and those became the design targets.

## The approach — an AI-assisted process

Rather than just *use* AI, I designed *with* it, end to end, and kept human judgment in charge of every decision:

- **Research & personas** — AI to cluster pain points and draft persona/journey hypotheses, which I then shaped and validated.
- **Feature prioritization & microcopy** — an impact/feasibility matrix and a consistent voice for buttons, errors, and reassurance states.
- **Low-fidelity → high-fidelity** — wireframes and polished screens in Visily, directed by prompts and refined by hand.
- **Conversational design** — the assistant's flows, recovery paths, and copy.

*Process is the product here: knowing what AI is good for — and where it shouldn't lead — is the skill these roles hire for.*

## The conversational AI assistant

The centerpiece. Instead of a dead-end error or a generic "contact support," a checkout assistant steps in **contextually** and is built to earn trust at the most anxious moment — payment.

- **Coupon assistance** — validates promo codes, and when one's expired, offers a real alternative instead of a wall.
  > "This coupon expired, but here's another offer you can use."
- **Payment recovery** — explains *what* went wrong, preserves checkout progress, and suggests an alternative method.
  > "Looks like that payment didn't go through. Your progress is saved and ready to continue."
- **Order & subscription tracking** — confirms status, generates receipts, explains billing.
  > "Your Premium subscription is now active. Need a copy of your receipt?"
- **Support escalation that keeps context** — when the assistant can't resolve it, it hands off to a human *with the conversation already transferred*, so the user never repeats themselves.

The throughline is trust: explain clearly, reassure, recover without losing progress, and escalate gracefully.
