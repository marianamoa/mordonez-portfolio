**One designer · a portfolio of brands · many marketplaces · every content type — all on strict global guidelines, in parallel, on deadline, and built to convert at thumbnail scale.**

A note on numbers: the retailers and the brand own the sales analytics, so this case study isn't about a conversion stat I can claim. It's about the *decisions* — what changes per brand, per marketplace, and per moment, and why. For a digital shelf, that judgment is the job.

## The challenge

Ferrero runs a whole portfolio of brands on the digital shelf — Nutella, Kinder, Tic Tac, Ferrero Rocher, Royal Dansk, Keebler, and more — each arriving with a thick global brand book and zero tolerance for going off-guideline. That same content then has to live across many marketplaces — Amazon, Walmart, Target, Kroger, Instacart and others — which each have different image rules, layouts, and shopper behavior. And it has to work twice: once at full size on the product page, and once as a postage-stamp thumbnail in a crowded search result, where most purchase decisions actually start.

On top of that, the work runs in parallel: multiple brands and portfolios moving at the same time, each with its own deadline — and none of them allowed to slip in quality or ship late.

My job: turn rigid brand guidelines into marketplace-specific shopping experiences that stay unmistakably on-brand, earn the click and the add-to-cart, and land on time across the whole queue.

## My role

Digital Shelf Designer for Ferrero's portfolio. I design and optimize the full product-page stack — hero images, basic content carousels, and enhanced (A+) content — plus seasonal campaigns and Amazon Brand Stores, across many brands and many marketplaces, several projects at a time.

- A **seasonal skin** — football-field blue, goalposts, "The Perfect Game Day Treat" — reads as Super Bowl instantly, even at thumbnail size.
- The **"$25K Each Week" Game Day sweepstakes** is a conversion lever tied to the moment, built into the design.
- Underneath the costume, **Kinder Bueno's wordmark, colors, and product truth stay sacred.**
- The judgment: a seasonal layer should be loud enough to signal the occasion and quiet enough to keep the brand instantly recognizable. That tension is the whole design problem.

## What I'd want you to notice

- I design the **whole funnel** (hero → carousel → enhanced → storefront), not isolated assets.
- I design for **the thumbnail first**, because that's where shoppers actually decide.
- I **translate constraints** — global brand books, each marketplace's rules, seasonal moments — into experiences that stay on-brand and still sell.
- I match the **register to the brand**: premium, playful, or everyday, deliberately.
- I do it **at volume and on deadline** — many brands across many marketplaces, several projects in flight at once, without letting quality slip.
